<article class="prose">

Formicary is a set of small, public experiments on whether the artifact-shaped techniques regulated industries spent thirty years building make AI-generated code last: maintainable as it changes, and still standing long after the agent that wrote it has moved on. It is applied research, run in the open, by someone who has had to live inside those standards.

I am Adrian Rossouw. For the last five years I was VP of Information Systems and Product at Renalytix, leading the technology organisation behind an FDA De Novo-authorized AI diagnostic, running a software group under ISO 13485 and ISO 27001, and shipping production EHR integrations with major US health systems. Before that, nearly seven years at NearForm in delivery architecture and technical direction. I have been audited under these standards, which is the qualification this site requires: not clause-by-clause authority, but knowing where the testable surface ends.

The site under-labels and over-delivers, on purpose. The numbers, when they come, will be ordinary numbers on questions almost everyone has an opinion about and very few have measured. The contribution is the measurement, not the discovery. I would rather publish honest results on obvious questions than novel speculation on hard ones.

**What it is**

<ul class="isnt-list">
<li>Applied research, in public. Working notes that change as data arrives.</li>
<li>A test of the artifact-shaped subset of regulated-software standards against AI agents, with measurement.</li>
<li>One experiment at a time. Gen 001 first. Another only if the first earns it.</li>
</ul>

</div>
<div>

**What it isn't**

<ul class="isnt-list">
<li>A product, a consultancy, or a newsletter on a schedule.</li>
<li>A claim that AI can do regulated software. That is a question for a notified body, not for me.</li>
<li>Clause-by-clause advice on the standards themselves.</li>
</ul>
